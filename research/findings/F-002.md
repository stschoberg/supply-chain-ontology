## Prediction
"BFO 2020's temporally qualified relations are hard to express well in OWL. Ownership transfers, roles that start
and end, and inventory moving between locations are where the encoding is weakest." We also expected IOF to have
time-qualified relations that SCO's BFO import lacks.

## What the files show
BFO's first-order axioms (ISO/IEC 21838-2, `common-logic/participation.cl`) put the time in the relation:

```
(forall (t a b) (iff (participates-in a b t) (has-participant b a t)))
```

BFO 2020 ships two OWL files. SCO imports `bfo-core.owl`; IOF Core imports `bfo.owl`. They use **the same IRIs**
for the shared relations, with different labels:

| IRI | `bfo-core.owl` (SCO) | `bfo.owl` (IOF) |
|-----|----------------------|-----------------|
| `BFO_0000056` | participates in | participates in at some time |
| `BFO_0000176` | continuant part of | continuant part of at some time |

`bfo.owl` also adds 24 "at all times" relations that `bfo-core.owl` lacks. Of the 64 relations in the two files, 24
have the same label in both, 16 share an IRI but are labeled differently, and 24 appear only in `bfo.owl`.

Both files' definitions agree that the binary relation means "at some time". From `bfo.owl`:
"b participates in p at some time =Def for some time t (p has participant b at t)". `bfo-core.owl`'s definition of
`continuant part of` says the same: "there is some time t such that … b continuant part of c at t".

SCRO uses the time-qualified relations directly: `participates in at some time` and `has participant at some time`
appear 11 times each in its restrictions, and `has continuant part at all times` and `has member part at all times`
4 times each.

## Attribution
`bfo-forced`, in OWL. OWL properties have two arguments, and both of BFO's official OWL files deal with the time
argument the same way: they quantify it away ("at some time", or "at all times" in `bfo.owl`). BFO's first-order
version keeps it. Any ontology that builds on BFO's OWL release inherits this. IOF made one choice on top: it imports
`bfo.owl`, so its labels say which quantifier applies and it gets the "at all times" variants.

## What it means
- **Costs:** a fact like "Acme bore a supplier role from 2023 to 2025" can't be stated with BFO's relations alone.
  `bearer of` says only that Acme bore it at some time. When something happened has to go through a process that
  occupies a temporal region, or through some reified pattern: ADR-0002's options 2 and 3.
- **Enforcement is `prose`:** the "at some time" reading lives only in labels and definitions. A reasoner treats
  `participates in at some time` as a plain binary property, so nothing checks the time quantifier.
- **For ADR-0002:** its context says BFO 2020 relations are "temporally unqualified in the OWL release". More
  precisely, they're qualified "at some time", and `bfo-core.owl` just doesn't say so in its labels. The design
  problem is the same, but the wording should change when the ADR is decided. Raised here, not resolved.
- **For SCO:** our `bearer of` and IOF's `has role` (a sub-property of `bearer of`) mean the same at-some-time
  relation, so the two ontologies agree here despite the different labels.
