## Prediction
"A shipment is a process": BFO's continuant/occurrent split would put the shipping on the occurrent side and the
goods on the continuant side, and the word "shipment" would name the shipping. This came from a general-knowledge
analysis, written without reading the IOF files.

## What the files show
`SupplyChain.rdf` makes shipment a defined class on the continuant side:

```
shipment EquivalentTo (material entity and (has role some shipment role))
```

The shipping is a separate class, linked to the shipment by participation:

```
supply chain transport process EquivalentTo (supply chain process
    and (has occurrent part some (transport process
        and (starts at some supply chain node) and (ends at some supply chain node)))
    and (has participant at all times some shipment))
```

The advance shipment notice is a third kind of thing, an information content entity. So one everyday "shipment"
spans three BFO categories: material entity (the goods), process (the moving), and generically dependent continuant
(the notice).

The axiom was first seen on 2026-10-09 while tracing the layers. The prediction above was written the next day,
independently of it.

## Attribution
`scro-specific`. BFO forces the *split*: continuant and occurrent are disjoint in BFO's OWL file, so no class can be
both the goods and the moving. BFO doesn't decide which side gets the *name*. SCRO chose to give "shipment" to the
goods, following logistics usage: its explanatory note quotes GS1, "a grouping of logistic and transport units
assembled and identified by the seller (sender) of the goods". Another BFO-based ontology could call the process
"shipment" without contradicting BFO.

## What it means
- **Buys:** the goods and the moving can't be confused in the data. A shipment has a weight; a transport process has
  a start and end. Querying "which shipments were delayed?" must go through the process, so nobody records delay as
  a property of a crate.
- **Costs:** one source record about a shipment becomes at least three individuals (goods, process, notice), plus
  the shipment role the goods bear. Anyone mapping data has to know which of the three a field describes.
- **For the argument:** this is the confound the method warns about. The prediction blamed BFO for a naming choice
  SCRO made. BFO forced the split; SCRO picked the name.
- **Lead for later:** IOF's own note on advance shipment notice says "the OWL equivalency axiom approximates the FOL
  definition but does not enforce identity between the shipment the ASN is about and the shipment participating in
  the arrival process." That's an IOF author saying the OWL loses what the first-order logic says, which belongs to
  the enforced vs. annotated principle.
